# The architecture `bank_init` creates

*The analysis below describes the shape as it stood on 6 September 2026 and measures it against five
real banks. Acting on it changed that shape the same day — a seeded bank is now 24 files, not 50.
The measurements are kept as they were taken; [what followed](#what-followed) says what changed and
what did not.*

One command wrote **14 directories and 50 files**: 38 copied from `starter/`, 11 section indexes
generated by code, and one root index carrying the project name.

This document describes that shape, then measures it against five banks that live with it —
`ebook_parser`, `tasman/research`, `idelo`, `focusreminder`, and a 378-document commercial bank whose
governance set has been running unchanged for long enough to show what happens next.

## Three kinds of thing

The fourteen directories are not fourteen of anything. They are three:

| | What it is | Size on day one | Who writes it afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dna/` | the bank's own law | 8 documents, 11 KB | copied once, then the bank's own |
| 11 sections | the shelves | 11 indexes, otherwise empty | the project |
| `flows/` | procedures and templates | 28 documents, 149 KB | copied once, rarely touched |

**`dna/`** is the constitution: `principles.md` (the root), `governance.md` (SSoT and ownership),
`frontmatter.md` (the field contract), `lifecycle.md`, `cross-references.md`, `glossary.md`,
`changelog.md`, and an index.

**The eleven sections** are created empty, each with a `README.md` saying *"Empty. A first document
of this kind is registered here."* The point is that the first document of any kind already has a
registered home rather than landing somewhere and being linked later — or never.

**`flows/`** is 92% of the starter set by volume. `feature-flow.md` alone is 44 KB.

*(Sizes here and below are content bytes. An earlier draft of this document reported `du` figures —
disk allocation, which for 24 small files overstates the text by half.)*

## The invisible axis: layers

Directories are what a person sees. The server sees something else: `layerOf()` takes the **first
path segment** and maps fourteen directories onto seven layers, each with a ranking weight.

| Layer | Directories | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| `knowledge` | product, domain, engineering, ops, system, services, research, strategies | **1.5** |
| `decision` | adr, use-cases, prd | 1.2 |
| `dna` · `flow` · `other` | dna · flows, processes, prompts · everything else | 1.0 |
| `delivery` | features, epics, tasks | **0.6** |
| `inbox` | _inbox | 0.2 |

Delivery is damped deliberately: in a mature bank it is around 80% of the corpus, and without the
damping routing would return feature briefs in place of the domain rule that governs them.

## The dependency tree

One root — `principles.md`, the only document with no `derived_from`. Everything else chains upward;
cycles are forbidden and checked. Orthogonal to that runs ownership: `canonical_for` declares which
fact belongs to which document, and `must_not_define` declares what a document is forbidden to
define.

Registration in a section index is not decoration but the reachability mechanism: a document no index
links to is caught by `unregistered-doc`. `bank_create` writes the file and registers it in one
operation, which is exactly why a hand-written document breaks the invariant and a call does not.

## The self-referential move

The validator carries no rules of its own. It **reads them out of `dna/`** — `parseContract` parses
the markdown tables and extracts the permitted values of `doc_kind`, `doc_function`, `status`,
whether `derived_from` is required, whether cycles are forbidden.

Hence the comment in `init.ts`: *the starter is a starting point, not a schema the server keeps
enforcing.* Copy `dna/` and it becomes yours. Add a value to a table and the validator accepts it
from that moment. A bank is judged by its own law.

## What is structurally sound

**Reachability is mechanical.** File and index entry arrive together or not at all.

**The tree is acyclic and single-rooted.** "What does this rest on" always terminates.

**The law travels with the corpus.** Clone the repository and you have both the documents and the
rules they are checked against. No external configuration, no server-side registry.

# Three weaknesses, measured

## 1. The starter set is half of a young bank

| Bank | Documents | `flows/` + `dna/` | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| idelo | 69 | 37 | **54%** |
| ebook_parser | 93 | 37 | 40% |
| tasman/research | 96 | 36 | 38% |
| focusreminder | 96 | 36 | 38% |

More than a third of a mature bank and more than half of a young one is copied scaffolding rather
than knowledge about the project. It enters search, statistics and repository size.

### And three sections were never used at all

| Section | ebook | research | idelo | focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| product · domain · engineering · adr | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| ops | — | — | 4 | 5 |
| use-cases | — | 13 | — | 4 |
| features | 5 | — | — | 11 |
| processes | 5 | — | — | — |
| **epics** | — | — | — | — |
| **prd** | — | — | — | — |
| **prompts** | — | — | — | — |

Four sections carry everything. `epics`, `prd` and `prompts` are index-only in all four banks, and
they drag eight more templates into `flows/` that nobody needed either.

## 2. The copied law drifts — and it already has

The starter files are byte-identical across banks on the day they are written, which means every
later improvement forks. This is not a prediction. Measured across the four banks above:

```
dna/principles.md      one hash in all four banks and the starter — five identical copies
flows/feature-flow.md  identical in three seeded banks; the fourth differs by 23 bytes
dna/frontmatter.md     identical in all four — and the starter is now ahead of every one of them
```

The last line happened during the session that wrote this document: adding the `anchors` row to the
starter contract made four banks stale, silently, in one edit.

### What it looks like after a long run

The 378-document commercial bank is the same lineage, older. Thirty of its thirty-six governance and
flow files are still byte-identical with the starter. Six have diverged — and one of the six is
`dna/governance.md`, the file that declares what the bank permits:

```
doc_kind      the bank lists 10 values; the current starter lists 14
doc_function  the bank lists  3 values; the current starter lists  9
```

The bank's practice has moved and its law has not. Counting its own documents against its own
declared contract:

```
378 documents
 29 carry a doc_kind the bank does not list      feature-support 9 · epic 8 · prompt 7 · process 5
 89 carry a doc_function it does not list        derived 49 · convention 21 · reference 9 · …
---
118 documents — 31% of the bank — outside the law it declares
```

Nothing here is junk. `epic`, `process`, `prompt`, `feature-support` and the four epic-registry
functions are precisely the values the current starter added; **91 of the 118 would be resolved by
refreshing one file.** The remaining 27 — `convention` twenty-one times, plus `plan`, `runbook`,
`guide`, `solution`, `execution`, `review_follow_up` — are vocabulary the bank invented in practice
and never wrote down.

That is the shape of the failure: not corruption, but a constitution copied once while the practice
kept moving. Every bank on this pattern is on the same trajectory; this one is simply further along.

## 3. The layer map belongs to the server, not the bank

The one place where "the bank declares its own rules" breaks. `BY_DIR` is a fixed table in
`layer.ts`. A bank that calls its section `scenarios` instead of `use-cases` silently falls into
layer `other`, loses the 1.2 weight, and produces **no finding at all** — validation does not look at
layers, they only affect ranking.

This is the trap that had to be spelled out by hand in the readtolearn migration prompt: the
repository name goes *under* the section, never above it, because a directory at the top of the path
decides the layer of everything beneath it.

## 4. The contract is parsed out of prose

`parseContract` looks for lines starting with `|` and takes backticked tokens as permitted values.
Elegant — one text, read by both a person and a machine. But reformatting a table can change what is
enforced, and nobody would notice. The contract has no contract of its own.

# What followed

All three were built the same day this was written — **B-08**, **B-09** and **B-10** in
[backlog.md](backlog.md). What a seeded bank looks like now:

```
             before   after
files            50      24
sections         11       8
flows/       28 docs   4 prose docs + a pointer to the templates
layers       server's   the bank's own table, defaulting to the server's
```

**The layers are declared in `dna/`.** A `Directory | Layer | Weight` table, parsed like the enum
tables beside it. The seeded table is identical in effect to the built-in map, so nothing changes
for a bank that keeps it and nothing breaks for a bank that has none. The hole in the central
principle is closed and the renaming trap is gone.

**Three sections are built on first use.** `bank_create` creates a missing section, its index and
its root-index entry in one operation, so the property that justified seeding them survives without
the empty ceremony.

**Only the templates are pointed at, not all of `flows/`.** This is where the original proposal was
wrong and the test suite said so: with the prose flows absent, routing could no longer answer a
question about the project's own procedure. The 44 KB feature flow is what people read; the 91 KB
of fill-in-the-blanks behind it is what nobody does. So the flows stay and the templates go, with
`bank_create` falling through to the shipped set and `--materialize-flows` for a project that means
to diverge.

## What none of it fixed

Weakness 2 above is only half addressed. Volume is handled — 91 KB per bank stops being copied —
but the divergence that actually cost something was in `dna/governance.md`, and `dna/` is still
copied on purpose: it is the law the bank is judged by, and it has to travel with the corpus. A bank
whose contract is a year behind still cannot see that, because it only ever reads its own.

That is B-04, and it is now the most valuable unbuilt item on the list. Not a byte diff — a
comparison of parsed contracts, answering "this bank permits four `status` values, that one three".
On the 378-document bank measured above it would have found, in one run, the single file whose
refresh resolves 91 of 118 documents.

Weakness 4 stands untouched: the contract is still parsed out of prose tables, and reformatting one
can still change what is enforced without anyone noticing.
